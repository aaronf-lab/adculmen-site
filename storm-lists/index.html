Why swath, not ZIP

A ZIP list pays postage on the houses the hail missed.

In the 39 ZIP codes around the June 19, 2026 Twin Cities storm, 13,741 of 279,071 address points (4.9%) sat inside the 1.00-inch band. For October 28 it was 2,081 of 105,915 (2.0%).1 A ZIP-based list spends 95 to 98 percent of its records and postage outside the hail. Ours holds only the records inside it.

1. FRS storm-event runs SEV-2026-06-19-MN and SEV-2026-10-28-MN, ZIP-versus-swath comparison (MnGeo address points tested against the same NOAA/NSSL MRMS MESH grid).

Questions

Before you mail.

Is every roof on the list damaged? No. A radar hail band is an estimate of hail size at the property. It tells you where to look, not what you will find.

Do I need to run the list through NCOA? Yes. To mail at presort prices, USPS requires a Move Update (NCOA) within 95 days of mailing, and CASS address matching for automation prices. Most mail platforms do both for you.

Can I resell it or share it? No. The license is for your own marketing only.

Anything Minnesota-specific? Roofers can't advertise or promise to pay or rebate an insurance deductible (Minn. Stat. § 325E.66). Keep your mail copy clean.

How fast do I get it? Within 24 hours of payment, by email.
