Where it came from

It started on the roof, not in a software catalog.

AD CULMEN™ began with years of standing on roofs after storms and sitting across the table in claims. Again and again, the same thing happened: a building took a real storm, and by the time anyone asked for proof, the evidence was gone, scattered or disputed. Dates were fuzzy. Records lived in someone's inbox, or nowhere at all. Owners who had done nothing wrong were left arguing about what happened to their own roofs.

The problem was never a lack of storms. It was a lack of a record. Nobody was keeping a clean, dated, building-by-building account that an owner, a lender and an engineer could all read the same way.

So Aaron M. Forsman built it himself. Not by buying a license or relabeling someone else's tool, but by doing the work by hand, building after building, until the method was dependable enough to repeat. That method became AD CULMEN™.

“The storm isn’t history. It’s inventory.”

The name is Latin, roughly “to the summit.” The roof is where the evidence lands first, and where it disappears fastest.

What it does

One clear storm record for every building you hold, finance or certify.

AD CULMEN™ turns a building's storm past into a document people can act on. It shows what reached the building and when, which claim windows may still be open (always verified against the policy), and what that means for the next budget. It does not decide fault or coverage, and it never claims a roof is damaged. It records the facts so the right people can make the call.

For owners

Know what your buildings have been through before a deadline passes. Plan roof capital on evidence, not guesswork, and walk into any conversation with a record already in hand.

For lenders

See storm exposure across a portfolio before you close, refinance or renew. Spot the buildings that carry unrecorded risk, and the ones that carry unclaimed value.

For engineers

Start from a documented storm history instead of reconstructing one. The record supports your judgment and never replaces it. Engineering opinions stay with you.

Proactive retention. Reactive prevention.
